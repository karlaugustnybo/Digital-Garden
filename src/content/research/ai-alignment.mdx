## Introduction

The alignment problem is often framed as ensuring that AI systems act in accordance with human intent. However, as systems scale, evaluating their outputs becomes increasingly difficult. This is the problem of *scalable oversight* [1].

We define the oversight gap $\Delta$ as:

$$
\Delta = P(\text{Model Correct}) - P(\text{Supervisor Correct})
$$

When $\Delta > 0$, the supervisor cannot reliably evaluate the model.

## Methodology

We employ a weak-to-strong generalization setup where a small model $M_{weak}$ supervises a larger model $M_{strong}$.

### Experimental Setup

Our loss function is defined as:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}} [ \ell(M_{strong}(x), M_{weak}(x)) + \lambda \mathcal{R}(M_{strong}) ]
$$

Where $\mathcal{R}$ is a regularization term ensuring the strong model doesn't simply mimic the weak model's errors.

## Results

We observed that $M_{strong}$ consistently outperforms $M_{weak}$ even when trained on labels generated by $M_{weak}$.

## Discussion

Our findings suggest that models can generalize beyond their supervision signal. This is promising for alignment, as it implies we may be able to bootstrap supervision for superintelligent systems.
